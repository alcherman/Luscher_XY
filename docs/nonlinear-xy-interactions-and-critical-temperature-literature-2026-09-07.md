# Nonlinear XY interactions and higher ordering temperatures: literature assessment

Search date: 2026-09-07 (America/Chicago). This is a targeted literature search, not an exhaustive novelty census. No simulations, data analysis, or implementation changes were performed.

The broad idea has substantial precedent: manipulating angular interactions and defect costs to change ordering temperatures predates the recent constraint-action studies. A specific smooth penalty at fixed small-angle stiffness remains a narrower comparison, but even the simple squared-cosine candidate discussed here belongs exactly to a published model family.

## 1. A finite angular penalty with no harmonic stiffness: the step model

Peter Olsson and Petter Holme, **Transition in the two-dimensional step model: A Kosterlitz–Thouless transition in disguise**, Physical Review B 63, 052407 (2001). [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0005414), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.63.052407).

Equation (1) uses V(delta) = -E sign(cos(delta)): the energy is -E for relative angles smaller than pi/2 and +E outside. Up to an additive constant this is a finite angular penalty of height 2E, with a perfectly flat interior. Their simulations and analysis support a BKT transition and a logarithmic vortex free energy generated by the loss of spin-wave entropy, despite a nonlogarithmic microscopic vortex energy. They distinguish this background entropy from vortex positional entropy.

This is a close statistical-mechanical predecessor to replacing infinite admissibility penalties by finite ones. It is discontinuous rather than smooth and is not an experimental materials-design demonstration. Its threshold is pi/2, so it does not directly address the broader vortex-allowed interior central to the present project. The paper cites earlier step-model work; 2001 is not its invention date.

## 2. Explicitly increasing Tc by suppressing vortices

Subodh R. Shenoy, **Enhancement and suppression of the transition temperature of a three-dimensional XY ferromagnet by control of vortex-loop fugacity**, Physical Review B 42, 8595 (1990). [Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.42.8595).

The abstract explicitly describes raising the transition temperature by suppressing vortex-loop fugacity and comparison with prior Monte Carlo work. Its additional loop-segment chemical potential is parameterized as mu = lambda k_B T. Thus fixed lambda is not a fixed physical energy penalty during heating. It is strong precedent for the defect-control idea, but not an identical fixed-Hamiltonian temperature scan or a smooth two-spin interaction.

Related antecedent: G. Kohring and R. E. Shrock, **Properties of a generalized 3D O(2) model with suppression/enhancement of vortex strings**, Nuclear Physics B 288, 397–418 (1987). [Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(87)90220-3). Monte Carlo and analytic phase-structure study with independently modified vortex weights.

Modern continuation: I. Maccari, N. Defenu, L. Benfatto, C. Castellani, and T. Enss, **Interplay of spin waves and vortices in the two-dimensional XY model at small vortex-core energy**, Physical Review B 102, 104505 (2020). [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.01526). Their smooth plaquette-current modification favors vortices in the studied parameter range; it is not a demonstration of enhanced Tc through a bond penalty. It illustrates the established separation of vortex-core physics from ordinary exchange, while accounting for nonvortex fluctuations.

## 3. The proposed squared-cosine potential is already in a studied family

Milan Žukovič, **XY model with antinematic interaction**, Physical Review E 99, 062112 (2019). [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1903.05586), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.99.062112).

Equation (1) studies -J1 cos(delta) - J2 cos(2 delta), with J1 > 0 and J2 < 0. The paper examines the square-lattice phase diagram, including ferromagnetic, antinematic, and canted regimes. It supplies a direct prior-art check for the smooth candidate; its emphasis is phase competition rather than optimizing Tc at fixed curvature.

The following mapping is an algebraic deduction from that Hamiltonian, not a new simulation result:

\[
J(1-\cos\delta)+U(1-\cos\delta)^2
=C-(J+2U)\cos\delta+\frac U2\cos(2\delta).
\]

Thus J1 = J+2U and J2 = -U/2. With total harmonic scale A = J+(5/2)U and normalized coefficient q = (J+2U)/A, the family lies at 4/5 < q <= 1 for J>0, U>=0; J=0 reaches q=4/5. Its bare curvature is A(5q-4)=J. Temperatures normalized by A convert to T/J by division by (5q-4). Published critical temperatures must be converted consistently before comparing at fixed J. No numerical enhancement is inferred here from the plotted phase boundary.

## 4. Materials work explicitly connects higher-order exchange to thermal stability

Alexey Kartsev, Mathias Augustin, Richard F. L. Evans, Kostya S. Novoselov, and Elton J. G. Santos, **Biquadratic exchange interactions in two-dimensional magnets**, npj Computational Materials 6, 150 (2020). [Published paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-020-00416-1), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04891).

The paper combines first-principles calculations, spin models, and Monte Carlo to study higher-order exchange in layered magnetic materials. Figure 3 compares thermal behavior with and without biquadratic exchange for chromium trihalides. Its section on magnetic stability explicitly discusses increased Curie temperatures and magnetic design. These are theoretical comparisons and agreement with measured material properties, not a controlled experiment that adds only the desired angular wall.

Equations (12)–(14) renormalize effective exchange and anisotropies. Therefore the reported effects do not isolate suppression of large angles at fixed small-angle stiffness. The isotropic sign convention also matters: the displayed Hamiltonian contains -K(S_i dot S_j)^2, whereas our positive-U candidate has a positive cos-squared coefficient compensated by stronger bilinear exchange.

Historical marker: H. A. Brown, **Heisenberg Ferromagnet with Biquadratic Exchange**, Physical Review B 4, 115 (1971). [Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.4.115). The abstract explicitly includes calculation of the Curie-temperature dependence on biquadratic exchange for spin-1 lattices. Only the abstract was checked; no quantitative result is attributed to it here.

## 5. Physical implementation remains a separate question

A. Mert Bozkurt, Jasper Brookman, Valla Fatemi, and Anton R. Akhmerov, **Double-Fourier engineering of Josephson energy-phase relationships applied to diodes**, SciPost Physics 15, 204 (2023). [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.04830).

This theoretical design method approximates arbitrary periodic energy-phase relations with Josephson circuits. It supports investigating a rounded angular wall as a target potential, but does not establish a higher-temperature ordered XY array. Thermal fluctuations of internal circuit modes, microscopic superconductivity, unwanted frustration, and the effective bond free energy at the proposed operating temperature would require separate assessment.

## Assessment for avoiding duplication

- Established: nonlinear exchange affects ordering temperatures; suppressing vortex fugacity can raise Tc; flat finite angular wells can support BKT behavior through entropic rigidity.
- Direct model overlap: the squared-cosine candidate is part of the published ferromagnetic-antinematic square-lattice XY family.
- Not established by this search: an experimental realization that smoothly penalizes large relative angles, retains the desired symmetry and spatial coupling pattern, and demonstrates a large equilibrium Tc increase at fixed small-angle stiffness.
- Potentially useful narrower question: compare Tc/V''(0), the full angle-dependent interaction, and finite-temperature stiffness for a physically justified smooth potential. This is a research question, not an assertion of novelty or authorization to simulate.

For any fixed physical Hamiltonian, beta multiplies both J and U. Comparisons must distinguish fixed U/J temperature scans from scans holding the dimensionless penalty beta U fixed. The latter change the physical penalty with temperature.

Searches covered step models, generalized/nonlinear XY potentials, ferromagnetic-antinematic exchange, independently tunable vortex costs, biquadratic-exchange Curie temperatures, and Josephson energy-phase engineering. Relevant full texts and equations were checked where indicated; the older Shenoy, Kohring–Shrock, and Brown entries were assessed through publisher abstracts. No complete forward/backward citation census, figure digitization, or independent numerical validation was performed. Failure to find the exact materials implementation is not proof of its absence.
