Constrained XY simulations
Transition curves and the 3D spin scaling dimension
Briefing for a collaborator’s talk | Results attained 13-15 October 2026. This is an exploratory numerical
report, not a precision universality determination. All quantities and figures below use the saved,
independently reviewed analyses; no new MC was generated for this report.

What these results support
The attained curves show a descriptive dimension-dependent approach toward the full tree-level prediction.
The β=0 transition is at ε/π greater than 1/2 in every studied dimension. In 3D, common-correlation-length
magnetization quotients give effective spin dimensions near Δ ≈ 0.52, compatible with the ordinary O(2)
Wilson-Fisher expectation. A calibrated combined statistical-plus-systematic error has not been established.

Simulation and analysis methods
Model, boundaries and sampling
Angles φ on periodic hypercubic boxes of volume V=Ld. The dimensionless action is S = -β Σlinks cos θ, with θ
= wrap(φj-φi) and the strict constraint |θ| < ε. Thus β=0 removes the cosine weight but preserves the hard
constraint; ε=π is the standard XY limit up to measure-zero configurations. L and N denote the same linear
box size.
One recorded hybrid MC cycle = one local Metropolis sweep (one proposal per site), four Wolff cluster
reflections, and one random-axis/sign unit-winding proposal. Four clusters are not four volume sweeps. Each
chain has its own seed; fresh pairs generally start in zero and unit axial winding backgrounds. Measurements
follow every cycle. Update/replay tests and full-field closure audits check implementation and recorded
observables; they do not prove equilibration or independent sampling.

Observables and finite-size transition definitions
Define m = V-1Σ exp(iφ). The O(2) Binder convention is U = 1 - ⟨|m|4⟩/[2⟨|m|²⟩²]. The second-moment ratio is
R=ξ/L = √(⟨S(0)⟩/⟨F⟩-1)/[2L sin(π/L)], with F averaged over axial minimal momenta. Ratios are formed from
ensemble moments, not averaged configuration-by-configuration ratios. Vortex density, all axial winding
histories, energy-like observables and update diagnostics are also recorded.
2D: local roots of R→0.7506912 and ηeff(L,2L)=-ln[⟨|m|²⟩2L/⟨|m|²⟩L]/ln2 → 1/4; exploratory shifts proportional
to (ln L+C)-2, shared infinite-volume limit and separate observable amplitudes. 3D/4D/5D: local U=1/4 roots.
Leading shift coordinates are respectively L-1/0.67169, L-2(ln L)-1/10, and L-5/2. Size cuts and correction terms
are tested, not assumed validated. In 5D the zero-mode Binder value is 1-π/4≈0.2146, so 1/4 is a shifted
fixed criterion.
Statistical treatment
Typical primary cuts discard cycles 0-1000 (3D/4D/5D curves and spin study); new 2D/endpoint analyses generally cut
through 2000. Retained histories are resampled in contiguous autocorrelation-aware blocks separately within starts, then
pooled by retained rows. Original 3D/4D positive curves use 1,000 bootstrap draws; 2D, 5D and spin analyses use 2,000.
Typical nominal block width is max(20, ceil[10 max τint]); the spin baseline doubles it and a further doubling is tested. Shared
draws preserve covariance of derived observables and overlapping size pairs. Narrow intervals remain conditional on mixing,
block choice, local windows and scaling laws.

3D at β=0: locating criticality
The target is the scaling dimension of the charge-one spin operator exp(iφ), not the magnetization critical
exponent βmag. In 3D, ⟨exp(iφ(x)) exp(-iφ(0))⟩ ∼ |x|-2Δ, with Δ=(1+η)/2. Correspondingly ⟨|m|²⟩ ∼ L-2Δ, up to
finite-size corrections and analytic backgrounds.
Figure 2. All four large sizes on one axis. Each ξ/L response is fitted independently; neither a shared crossing nor a target
critical R is imposed. Curves may have different finite-size crossings. The near-common intersection is useful evidence, not
proof of an exact thermodynamic critical coupling.
Common-coupling quotient method
For each pair L:2L, solve RL(q*)=R2L(q*) at the same q=ε/π. Then evaluate Δeff=-ln[⟨|m|²⟩2L,q*/⟨|m|²⟩L,q*]/(2
ln2). This avoids matching magnetizations at different ε or imposing a universal Binder/R value. The final 120
trajectories refine common knots with fine interior spacing 0.000125; L=48,64 chains have 20,000 cycles.
Noisy knot-by-knot interpolation can yield multiple crossings. The displayed results instead use weighted
local quadratic responses for R and ⟨|m|²⟩ over q=0.800500-0.801500, with original joint bootstrap draws.
Inner-window, linear, cut, block and start-parity alternatives are retained. A required root must be physical,
unique in its window and present in at least 99% of bootstrap draws.

Reading Δ and answering questions
Figure 3. A direct slope visualization at exactly measured q=0.80075: √⟨|m|²⟩ ∼ L-Δ_eff. Correlated fit over L=24,32,48,64 gives
Δ_eff=0.51942 ± 0.00795 stat (one SD); conditional MC95 [0.50398,0.53505]. This fixed-q near-critical slope excludes
uncertainty in thermodynamic criticality and scaling corrections.
How to quote the result
Suggested talk wording: “At β=0, finite-size magnetization quotients and a direct magnetization slope
give Δ near 0.52, compatible with the standard 3D O(2) Wilson-Fisher CFT. The statistical precision is a few
10-3 for selected pair quotients, but finite-size and response-model uncertainty are not yet calibrated.” Do
not turn this into Δ=0.52±0.01 as a formal confidence interval or quote an invented σsys.
Conditional correlated extrapolations with a fixed Wilson-Fisher-informed correction exponent ω=0.789 give
0.52321±0.00346 stat (minimum pair L=8) and 0.51834±0.00616 (minimum L=12); an inner-linear
alternative gives 0.52057±0.00640. These are sensitivity examples, not a selected final CFT value. The raw
unsmoothed 32:64 quotient is 0.52593±0.00572, with start-parity sensitivity; local regression changes the
estimator. Literature comparison: Δ=0.519088(22) from conformal bootstrap [1], which also tabulates the
MC result 0.519050(40). Neither was imposed as the measured Δ.

Questions and numerical context
Qualifications worth saying aloud
What do the pale bars mean? A descriptive union across tested cuts, thresholds, interpolants and scaling
models, including amplified MC noise and some rejected-model diagnostics; not a systematic standard
deviation or total confidence interval.
What remains unresolved? Absolute BKT accuracy: the 2D q=1 dark interval misses the known 1.1199
benchmark [2], although its wider union includes it. The 5D q=1 leading size fit rejects and is diagnostic only.
Low independent-block counts, including as few as seven in some 5D starts, and start/half-history flags
remain material.
Why can order survive at β=0? The angle constraint survives heating and can create entropic rigidity.
Vortices being permitted at ε>π/2 does not imply their macroscopic proliferation. In 2D say “massless/BKT
phase,” not spontaneous magnetization.
